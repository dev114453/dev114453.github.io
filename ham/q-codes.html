Ham Radio Q Codes

Q codes are three-letter abbreviations that started in Morse code and are now used on voice too. Sent with a question mark, a Q code asks a question; without one, it is a statement.

CodeAs a questionAs a statement
QRLIs the frequency in use?The frequency is in use.
QRMIs there interference?There is interference (usually from other stations).
QRNAre you troubled by static?I am troubled by static / atmospheric noise.
QROShall I increase power?Increase power.
QRPShall I decrease power?Decrease power; also means low-power operating.
QRQShall I send faster?Send faster.
QRSShall I send more slowly?Send more slowly.
QRTShall I stop sending?I am stopping transmission / closing down.
QRVAre you ready?I am ready.
QRXWhen will you call me again?Wait; I will call you again.
QRZWho is calling me?You are being called by…
QSBAre my signals fading?Your signals are fading.
QSLCan you acknowledge receipt?I acknowledge receipt; also a confirmation card.
QSOCan you communicate with…?A contact (conversation) between stations.
QSYShall I change frequency?Change to another frequency.
QTHWhat is your location?My location is…

On the exam

The Technician pool asks about QRM, QSY and similar codes, and the General and Extra pools go further with CW procedures. Learning the dozen codes above covers most of what you will hear on the air.

Getting your license? Try the free Technician practice test, or study on your phone with the Ham Radio Technician Exam app.
