NATO Phonetic Alphabet for Ham Radio

Radio operators spell letters with code words so they are understood through noise and accents. Amateur radio uses the international (ITU / NATO) phonetic alphabet, the same one used in aviation and at sea.

How hams use it

Give your call sign phonetically when signals are weak or when someone asks for a repeat. For example, the call sign KD9XYZ is spoken "Kilo Delta Niner X-ray Yankee Zulu". FCC rules (§97.119) require you to identify with your call sign at least every 10 minutes during a contact and at the end; using the phonetic alphabet is encouraged but not required.

The Technician exam asks about using the phonetic alphabet, so it is worth learning by heart.
